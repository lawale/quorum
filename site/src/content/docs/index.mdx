<section class="not-content">
## How a request moves

<ol class="q-track">
  <li><strong>Your app creates a request</strong><p>One call with a type and a JSON payload. The caller is the maker.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Quorum finds the policy</strong><p>Stages, thresholds, who may act and when to expire, all read from data.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Checkers sign</strong><p>Self-approval, roles, permissions, scope and duplicate votes are checked before a signature lands.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Your app acts on the decision</strong><p>Approved, rejected or expired arrives by webhook, event stream or poll. Your app performs the write.</p></li>
</ol>
</section>

<section class="not-content">
## The rules are a document, not a deploy

<div class="q-split">
  <dl class="q-notes">
    <div><dt>Stages in order</dt><dd>Each with its own threshold, allowed roles or permissions, and rejection rule.</dd></div>
    <div><dt>Scope</dt><dd>Only the compliance officers in the maker's branch may sign the second stage. Attributes come from your identity provider's claims or your gateway's headers.</dd></div>
    <div><dt>Bypass with a velocity limit</dt><dd>Amounts under 1,000 skip review, but the fourth one in a day comes back for a human to look at. Limits stop a threshold from being gamed.</dd></div>
    <div><dt>Expiry</dt><dd>A request nobody decides on does not stay open forever.</dd></div>
  </dl>

<section class="not-content">
## What is included

<dl class="q-rows">
  <div><dt>Layered permission guards</dt><dd>The maker cannot approve their own request. Per-request eligible reviewers, per-stage roles and permissions, reviewer scoping by attributes such as branch, an optional authorization endpoint you control, and one vote per checker per stage. Evaluated in order on every decision.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>An audit trail you can hand to an auditor</dt><dd>Every create, approve, reject, cancel, bypass and expiry is permanently logged with actor, timestamp and details. One place to answer who approved this, and when.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Webhooks that cannot be lost</dt><dd>Deliveries are written to an outbox in the same transaction as the state change, signed with HMAC-SHA256 and retried with backoff. A review_requested event tells your application exactly who is allowed to act next, so you can notify them.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Approval screens for any front end</dt><dd>Three Lit Web Components: an approval panel, a request list and a stage progress bar. Shadow DOM isolation, theming through CSS custom properties, live updates over Server-Sent Events. Load them from a script tag or npm.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Your identity provider stays in charge</dt><dd>Validate JWTs against any OIDC provider's JWKS endpoint with claim mapping and automatic key rotation, or trust headers from your gateway. Quorum never becomes a user directory.</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Built to be operated</dt><dd>PostgreSQL or SQL Server, with everything in its own schema so Quorum can share your database. Prometheus metrics, a health endpoint that reports its version, multi-arch Docker images, an OpenAPI contract validated against the real server in tests, and official Go, TypeScript and .NET clients built on it.</dd></div>
</dl>
</section>
